## 1 · The finding

The Estate today shows **"Rules in force"** and **"Checks enforcing them"** as counts, alongside enforcements, violations, access events, exports blocked, retention holds, and destruction attestations [recalled, from the filed Govern module spec].

A DPO reading those counts cannot tell which governance is **mechanically enforced** — a check that runs, fails closed, and produces a receipt — and which is **attested** — a policy statement a person affirms, with no machine standing behind it. Those are radically different assurances presented in one number.

This is the product-facing form of the matrix's own R0 [fact, matrix v0.2 §2]: *a condition that cannot be verified from the record is not a condition, it is a hope.* An Estate that conflates the two invites a DPO to defend, in front of a regulator, a figure that is partly machinery and partly good intentions — without knowing which part is which.

The defect is not that attested governance is illegitimate. Some governance is irreducibly human: an attestation document signed by a CEO cannot be a running check, and should not pretend to be. The defect is that the surface does not say so.

---

## 2 · RULED — the Estate distinguishes enforcement class

**2.1 · Every rule in the Rules Record carries an enforcement class**, displayed, one of:

- **Enforced** — a check runs, fails closed, and emits a receipt. Carries its enforcement count and violation count, as today.
- **Attested** — held by a recorded human act (a signature, an uploaded attestation, a countersign). Carries who attested, when, and the artifact reference. No enforcement count, because there is no check to count.
- **Monitored** — measured and reported, but does not block. Carries its observation count and states plainly that it does not stop the action.

**2.2 · The headline stat splits.** "Checks enforcing them" is replaced by two figures with one line of plain language between them — how much of the governance in force is machinery, and how much is human attestation. The DPO's own question becomes answerable on the landing: *how much of my governance actually fires?*

**2.3 · No class is presented as superior.** Attested governance is not a deficiency to be remediated — the three-party Sponsor succession is deliberately attested, and converting it to machinery would defeat its purpose [recalled, Govern spec + change order A3]. The surface reports the class; it does not editorialize.

**2.4 · Class is derived, never hand-set.** A rule's class follows from the Rules Taxonomy [fact, change order A3]: Class S rails and machine-checked Class O rules are **Enforced**; governance held by attestation documents is **Attested**; anything measured-but-non-blocking is **Monitored**. The Estate reads the class from the record. It is never a field someone types, which would make the honesty claim itself unverifiable.

**2.5 · "See the record" behaves per class.** Enforced → the check's live result and receipt. Attested → the attestation artifact, its signatories, and its date. Monitored → the observation log with its non-blocking status stated.

---

## 3 · Why this is worth building

**It is the product's promise pointed at itself.** Akki's thesis is that governance should be enforced and provable rather than asserted in a policy document [fact, product spec framing]. An Estate that cannot distinguish its own enforced rules from its own asserted ones fails that thesis on its most-read screen.

**It is cheap.** A class on an existing row, a split on an existing stat card, and per-class routing on an existing link. No new module, no new flow, no new contract [inferred].

**It survives contact with a regulator.** A DPO who can say "eleven of our fourteen rules are enforced by checks that fail closed, and here are their receipts; the other three are attested, and here are the signed documents" is in a materially stronger position than one presenting a single number. That is the defensibility the product exists to produce.

## 4 · Not adopted

- **No conformance-matrix feature.** The build's matrix stays on the build side. The product does not acquire a matrix screen, a matrix vocabulary, or a matrix report.
- **No build-discipline exposure.** Users see governance state, never the platform's own development conformance.
- **No imported vocabulary.** "Incompletely specified" does not enter the product. Its customer-facing equivalent already exists as Registry's plain **"not yet measured"** and stays as it is.
- **No remediation pressure.** Nothing in the surface urges converting Attested rules into Enforced ones.

## 5 · One consistency check (rides Prove's next spec pass)

The product carries a two-class convention on displayed figures — **measured / estimated** [recalled, Registry + Prove specs]. The build carries three — fact / recalled / inferred [fact, house rule 11].

At the Prove specification's next pass, check whether two classes are sufficient on Answer Cards, or whether an inferred figure is currently rendering as *estimated* when it is materially different from a measured-with-uncertainty figure. This is a **check, not a ruling** — it may well close as "two is correct." It is recorded so the question is asked once rather than assumed [inferred].

Related and already correct, recorded for completeness: the GAP register's discipline — name the absence rather than hide it — is already the product's behaviour in Registry's "not yet measured" hatching. No change owed.
